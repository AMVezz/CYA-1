# Meeting notes – Navy Information Professional officer recruiter

**Date:**  April 25 2026  
**Attendees:**  Aiden Vezzetti, Navy officer recruiter

I scheduled a one‑on‑one call with LT Mohamed Benallal, a Navy Reserve Information Professional officer recruiter.  Below is a summary of the discussion and my personal notes.

## Questions and responses

1. **What do Information Professional (IP) officers do?**  
   The recruiter explained that IP officers are technical leaders within the Information Warfare community.  They plan, operate and secure the Navy’s global networks and combat systems.  Key responsibilities include defending Navy networks against cyber threats, operating global telecommunications and space systems, ensuring resilient command and control through secure communications, and collaborating with other services and coalition partner.  He emphasised that officers often lead teams of enlisted information technicians and coordinate with experts across multiple disciplines.

2. **How does the Information Professional role differ from other technology specialities (e.g., Cyber Warfare Officer or Cryptologic Warfare Officer)?**  
   IP officers focus on communications networks, information systems and knowledge management.  Cyber Warfare officers concentrate on offensive and defensive cyber operations, while Cryptologic Warfare officers specialise in signals intelligence and electronic warfare.  All fall under the Information Warfare umbrella but have distinct mission sets.  Choosing between them depends on whether you prefer network operations, cyber offence/defence or intelligence.

3. **What are the educational and professional requirements?**  
   He noted that candidates must have a bachelor’s degree (preferably in computer science, cybersecurity, electrical engineering or a related STEM field) and a minimum GPA of 3.0.  Applicants must also meet age and citizenship criteria and be eligible for a TS/SCI clearance.  Prior cyber‑related experience and certifications such as CISSP or Security+ are highly desirable.  Successful applicants attend Officer Development School, the Information Warfare Basic Course and the IP Basic Course.

4. **What does the application and selection process look like?**  
   The process starts with submitting a package that includes transcripts, a resume highlighting technical and leadership experience, and letters of recommendation.  Applicants must complete the Officer Aptitude Rating (OAR) exam and pass medical and security screenings.  Selected candidates attend Officer Candidate School and commission as Ensigns.

5. **How are promotions and career progression handled?**  
   I asked how technical performance influences promotions.  He pointed to recent changes in Navy policy that emphasise technical mastery over volunteer activities for enlisted promotions and said that a similar focus applies to officers.  Performance evaluations prioritise mastery of one’s technical specialty, completion of required qualifications, and leadership performance.  Opportunities for advanced education and postgraduate study exist throughout an IP officer’s career.

6. **What advice do you have for a student exploring this path?**  
   LT Benallal encouraged me to build a strong foundation in networking, cybersecurity and leadership.  He recommended seeking internships, participating in cyber competitions and obtaining relevant certifications.  He stressed the importance of talking to multiple recruiters and officers to gain diverse perspectives before committing.

## Personal observations

- The conversation highlighted that the IP officer role aligns closely with my interests in cyber defense, network engineering and leadership.  
- I was impressed by the emphasis on continuous professional development and the availability of advanced training and graduate‑level education.  
- Preparing for the call forced me to refine my résumé and articulate my technical experiences clearly; the recruiter’s feedback will help me strengthen future applications.  
- I realised that the military’s focus on technical excellence matches my desire to become a subject‑matter expert.

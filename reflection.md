# Reflection on career‑aligned networking experience

### Activity description

For the Choose Your Own Adventure assignment I chose to network with officer recruiters to explore the United States Navy’s Information Professional (IP) officer program.  This activity involved scheduling a meeting with LT Mohamed Benallal, a Navy Reserve recruiter, researching the IP role, preparing questions and discussing the commissioning process.  I also updated my résumé to highlight my technical and leadership experience and reviewed official sources on Navy promotion policies and IP responsibilities.  The meeting itself took place on April 25 2026 and lasted about forty‑five minutes.  We covered topics such as duties of IP officers, differences between Information Warfare specialities, eligibility requirements, application steps, and career progression.  

### Key decisions

Several technical and professional decisions influenced this experience.  First, I decided to focus on the Information Professional speciality rather than other technical roles.  Research indicated that IP officers are responsible for planning, operating and securing the Navy’s global networks and communications systems which aligns closely with my interest in networking and cyber‑defense.  Second, I prioritised understanding the qualifications and promotion criteria.  The Navy’s April 2026 memo emphasising technical mastery in promotion boards convinced me that building deep expertise would be critical.  Consequently, I chose to pursue CompTIA Security+ certification before applying and to refine my résumé to highlight technical achievements.  Third, I structured my questions to gain actionable insight: I asked about differences between IP and other Information Warfare roles, sought advice on competitive preparation, and clarified the application timeline.

### Contributions

This was an individual activity, but I leveraged resources and sought input from multiple sources.  I independently conducted research, compiled the meeting agenda and arranged the appointment.  During the meeting I actively led the discussion, ensuring that all prepared questions were addressed while remaining receptive to new information.  After the meeting I shared my notes with a mentor in the WSU Cyber Security Club to gain feedback on my understanding and to help other students who might be interested in military service.  I also documented the experience in this repository to provide a resource for peers exploring similar paths.

### Quality assessment

Overall, I believe the experience was productive.  My preparation allowed me to engage the recruiter in a focused conversation that yielded specific answers and advice.  I gained clarity on the IP role’s responsibilities defending networks, operating communications systems, and ensuring command and control resilience and on qualifications such as STEM degrees, GPA requirements and security clearances.  I was encouraged by the Navy’s commitment to continuous professional development through specialised training courses.  If I were to repeat this activity I would schedule informational interviews with officers currently serving in the role to obtain a first‑hand perspective on day‑to‑day duties and work–life balance.  I would also prepare more targeted questions about balancing civilian and reserve careers since I am considering the reserve component.

### Career alignment

This experience directly advanced my short‑ and long‑term goals.  Short‑term, it helped me identify concrete steps to strengthen my candidacy: completing my degree, earning Security+ certification, and gaining leadership experience.  Long‑term, it clarified how an Information Professional commission would align with my desire to lead technical teams and protect national infrastructure.  I learned that IP officers play a vital role in defending Navy networks and enabling mission success, and that the Navy values technical mastery over ancillary activities when considering promotions.  This reinforced my commitment to deepening my technical expertise.  Whether I ultimately pursue a military commission or a civilian cyber‑defense role, the skills and insights gained from this networking experience will be invaluable.

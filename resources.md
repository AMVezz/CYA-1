# Research notes and sources

To prepare for my meeting and understand the Information Professional (IP) career path, I reviewed the following resources:

## Navy promotion policy emphasising technical mastery

A *Task & Purpose* article from April 2026 described a memo from the Chief of Navy Personnel.  The memo directs promotion boards to prioritize sailors’ technical job skills rather than volunteer activities or collateral duties【290328486769435†L87-L115】.  It explains that by fiscal year 2028 the promotion system will reward sailors who demonstrate technical mastery in fields like aviation, air traffic control, electronics and special warfare.  This change underscores the Navy’s commitment to technical excellence and influenced my decision to pursue a role where technical expertise is paramount.

## Information Professional officer responsibilities

A 2025/2026 Navy Reserve recruiting post explained that Information Professional officers lead cyberspace defensive operations, communications and knowledge management.  They plan, operate and secure the Navy’s global networks and combat systems【677734531552607†L36-L75】.  Key responsibilities include:

- **Defending Navy networks against cyber threats through defensive cyberspace operations**【677734531552607†L52-L60】.  
- **Operating and maintaining global telecommunications and space systems**【677734531552607†L54-L59】.  
- **Ensuring resilient command and control across naval operations through secure and reliable communication networks**【677734531552607†L56-L60】.  
- **Collaborating with military branches, government agencies and coalition partners**【677734531552607†L60-L61】.  
- **Focusing on cyber operations, command and control, information systems and assurance, knowledge management, and space/communications systems**【677734531552607†L63-L74】.  

This description helped me formulate specific questions for the recruiter and confirm that the IP role aligns with my skills and interests.

## Commissioning requirements and qualifications

The same resource outlined that applicants must be U.S. citizens aged 18–42, hold a bachelor’s degree in a relevant field, maintain at least a 3.0 GPA, and be eligible for a TS/SCI security clearance【677734531552607†L102-L117】.  It also highlighted the importance of certifications such as CISSP and Security+ to strengthen an application【677734531552607†L107-L110】.  Understanding these requirements guided my preparation and helped me identify areas (e.g., certifications) to pursue before applying.
